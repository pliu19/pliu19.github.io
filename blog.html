Teaching a Search Engine What You Actually Meant

October 2025 Search & LLMs

Job search queries are short, ambiguous, and rarely say what the seeker really wants. Keyword matching gives up quickly. Over the last two years my team replaced that layer with language models — and learned that the hard part isn't the model, it's the reward.

#LLM #Search #LinkedIn
Read More →

Starting point: LLMs in the production stack

In Powering Job Search at Scale (CIKM 2025) we brought LLM-based query understanding into LinkedIn's production job matching system — interpreting seeker intent from queries that are often three words long, at full request volume.

Collapsing the cascade

Large search systems accumulate a cascade of task-specific query-understanding components, each with its own model, data, and failure modes. In A Unified Structured Query Understanding Framework for Industrial Semantic Search we replaced that cascade with a single Small Language Model doing constrained generation. The blocker was annotation, so we built Query Illuminator, which does double duty: teacher model for auto-annotation and distillation, and surrogate judge for evaluation at a scale humans can't cover. It runs today in both Job Search and People Search, inside strict latency limits on constrained GPU capacity. (paper)

The lesson: reward design beats optimizer choice

Our most recent work generates portable queries — terms that drop seeker-specific identifiers but keep the qualifications that generalize. Training it with RLAIF surfaced something we didn't expect: policy optimization is very good at finding holes in LLM-as-judge rubrics. It collapsed into degenerate verbatim copying that scored well and helped nobody. A deterministic, rule-based reward floor fixed it. We also found the training-time reward model inflated apparent gains by 2.4×. For critic-free optimizers, how you write the reward matters more than which algorithm you reach for. (paper)

Graphs at LinkedIn: From LiGNN to LinkSAGE

August 2024 Graph Learning

Our paper "LiGNN: Graph Neural Networks at LinkedIn" received the Best Paper Award at KDD 2024. It describes the platform side of graph learning here. The follow-up question I spent the next year on was narrower: how do you get graph signal into job matching without every team retraining a GNN?

#GNN #KDD #JobMatching
Read More →

LiGNN

LiGNN covers how graph neural networks are trained and served at LinkedIn scale — the infrastructure behind a large share of the recommendations on the platform. I contributed on the job matching side.

LinkSAGE

LinkSAGE: Optimizing Job Matching Using Graph Neural Networks (KDD 2025) takes a deliberately decoupled approach: GNN training is separated from the downstream ranking models. The graph model learns member and job representations once; many downstream models consume them without anyone retraining the graph model. That separation is what made graph signal practical to adopt across the matching stack rather than in one model.

And the plumbing

None of it ships without the boring part. A Scalable and Efficient Signal Integration System for Job Matching (KDD 2025) is about exactly that: getting heterogeneous signals into matching models efficiently enough that iterating on them stays cheap.

Filter Bubbles, and What Happens When You Hand Users the Dial

May 2024 Social Computing

My Ph.D. work was about filter bubbles in news recommender systems: how they form, who they affect most, and whether giving users explicit control over the algorithm actually changes what they read.

Not everyone bubbles the same way

The interaction between political typology and filter bubbles in news recommendation algorithms (WWW 2021) looked at the same recommender across readers of different political typologies. The effect isn't uniform — which matters, because mitigations designed for an average user won't land evenly.

Giving users the controls

How Does Empowering Users with Greater System Control Affect News Filter Bubbles? (ICWSM 2024) asks the obvious follow-up: if the system exposes control over recommendations, does the bubble loosen?
