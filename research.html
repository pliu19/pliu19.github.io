Research

At LinkedIn, I work on the machine learning behind the job marketplace — connecting members to the opportunities worth their attention, whether they type a query or never search at all. Three questions organize most of it:

  • What does this member actually want? — inferring intent from an explicit search query, and inferring it from a member's profile and activity when there is no query. This is where language models have changed the most: interpreting and structuring queries, and building representations of a member rich enough to retrieve against.
  • Which results genuinely match that intent? — retrieval and ranking across a very large and constantly changing set of jobs and members, through graph-based representations, embedding retrieval, and semantic relevance models.
  • How do we model it, and make it run at billion scale in real time? — picking the right technique for each piece — large and distilled language models, graph neural networks, reinforcement learning from engagement signals — and then getting it to survive production: graphs with billions of nodes and edges, strict latency budgets, and limited GPU capacity. Decoupling training from serving, distillation, and signal-integration systems are what keep iteration cheap enough to continue.

In practice the hard part is rarely the model. It is deciding what the system should optimize for, and then checking that the thing you measured is the thing you actually wanted.

Before LinkedIn, my Ph.D. research centred on filter bubbles in news recommender systems — the subject of my dissertation — alongside natural language processing in social networks, machine learning for microscopy and cell biology, and the analysis of model sensitivity to input perturbations.

Selected Work at LinkedIn

First-author papers. Each one corresponds to a system that shipped to production in LinkedIn Job Search or Job Matching.

  • Designing Reward Signals for Portable Query Generation: A Case Study in Industrial Semantic Job Search
    Ping Liu, Qianqi Shen, Jianqiang Shen, Wenqiong Liu, Rajat Arora, Yunxiang Ren, Chunnan Yao, Dan Xu, Baofen Zheng, Wanjun Jiang, Andrii Soviak, Kevin Kao, Jingwei Wu, Wenjing Zhang
    KDD Workshop on AI Agent for Information Retrieval, Agent4IR (2026)  ·  arXiv
    An end-to-end RLAIF framework that generates portable job-search queries — terms that abstract away seeker-specific identifiers while preserving generalizable qualifications. The reward surface turns out to be highly adversarial: policy optimization exploits flaws in LLM-as-judge rubrics and collapses into degenerate verbatim copying. A deterministic rule-based reward floor corrects it. For critic-free optimizers, reward shaping dominates the choice of algorithm — RLOO and REINFORCE++ resist reward hacking natively, while GRPO's group-relative advantage normalization proves uniquely sensitive to spurious signal.

  • A Unified Structured Query Understanding Framework for Industrial Semantic Search
    Ping Liu, Qianqi Shen, Jianqiang Shen, Chunnan Yao, Kevin Kao, Rajat Arora, Dan Xu, Baofen Zheng, Yunxiang Ren, Benjamin Le, Ali Hooshmand, Igor Lapchuk, Juan Bottaro, Raghavan Muthuregunathan, Caleb Johnson, Liangjie Hong, Jingwei Wu, Wenjing Zhang
    KDD (2026)  ·  arXiv
    Replaces the usual cascade of disparate, task-specific query-understanding components with a single Small Language Model doing schema-constrained generation, which cuts the maintenance overhead and the inconsistent behaviour that cascade picks up on long-tail queries. Introduces Query Illuminator, which serves double duty as a teacher for auto-annotation and distillation and as a surrogate judge where human labels are scarce. Deployed in LinkedIn Job Search under strict latency limits on constrained GPU capacity, with a cross-domain case study on People Search.

  • Powering Job Search at Scale: LLM-Enhanced Query Understanding in Job Matching Systems
    Ping Liu, Jianqiang Shen, Qianqi Shen, Chunnan Yao, Kevin Kao, Dan Xu, Rajat Arora, Baofen Zheng, Caleb Johnson, Liangjie Hong, Jingwei Wu, Wenjing Zhang
    CIKM (2025)  ·  paper
    The first step toward the unified framework above. Job search had been reading queries through a set of task-specific named-entity models — brittle, expensive to maintain, and slow to follow a taxonomy as it moves. This replaces them with a single LLM that reads the query and profile attributes together, so a short, ambiguous query is interpreted in light of who is asking. Improved relevance in online A/B tests while removing a large amount of system complexity.

  • LinkSAGE: Optimizing Job Matching Using Graph Neural Networks
    Ping Liu, Haichao Wei, Xiaochen Hou, Jianqiang Shen, Shihai He, Kay Qianqi Shen, Zhujun Chen, Fedor Borisyuk, Daniel Hewlett, Liang Wu, Srikant Veeraraghavan, Alex Tsun, Chengming Jiang, Wenjing Zhang
    KDD (2025)  ·  paper
    A graph neural network framework over LinkedIn's job marketplace graph — billions of nodes and edges, members and jobs carrying their attributes. Inductive learning on a heterogeneous, evolving graph with an encoder-decoder GNN, and the part that makes it practical: GNN training is decoupled from the existing ranking DNNs, so graph signal reaches them by transfer learning instead of constant joint retraining. A nearline inference system serves the encoder, which keeps online latency down and avoids standing up real-time GNN infrastructure.

  • A Scalable and Efficient Signal Integration System for Job Matching
    Ping Liu, Rajat Arora, Xiao Shi, Benjamin Le, Qianqi Shen, Jianqiang Shen, Chengming Jiang, Nikita Zhiltsov, Priya Bannur, Yidan Zhu, Liming Dong, Haichao Wei, Qi Guo, Luke Simon, Liangjie Hong, Wenjing Zhang
    KDD (2025)  ·  paper
    STAR (Signal Integration for Talent And Recruiters) pairs LLMs, which read the text of member profiles and job postings, with GNNs, which capture the relationships between them and blunt cold-start through network effects. Aimed at three failure modes of job matching — cold-start, filter bubbles, and bias in candidate-job pairing. As much about the industrial paradigms that keep embeddings maintainable in production, adaptive sampling and version management among them, as about the models.
